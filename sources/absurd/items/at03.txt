@ Literature & Books | The Giant Devil's Bible | Literature & Books, Medieval History, Eccentrics
The largest surviving medieval manuscript, the Codex Gigas, weighs 75 kilograms and is famous for its full-page portrait of the Devil.
- Its Latin name means "giant book"
- It has 310 parchment leaves, probably calfskin
- It was made in 13th-century Bohemia
- Besides the Bible it holds histories, medical texts and a calendar
- Legend claims a monk wrote it in one night with the Devil's help

@ Literature & Books | The Book Nobody Can Read | Literature & Books, Mysteries & Legends
Yale University holds a 15th-century illustrated book written in an unknown script that nobody has ever deciphered, despite a century of work by professional codebreakers.
- It's called the Voynich manuscript, after the dealer who bought it in 1912
- Tests date the parchment to the 1400s
- Its sections show plants, star charts, bathing scenes and recipes
- None of the plants depicted match known species
- Yale says it isn't even certain the text is a code at all

@ Military History | The War Started by Throwing Men Out a Window | Military History, Law & Government
In 1618 Protestant nobles in Prague tried two royal officials and their secretary, then threw all three out of a castle window; none was seriously hurt, and the Thirty Years' War followed.
- The dispute was over closed Protestant chapels
- Defenestration means throwing someone out of a window
- The men fell from the council room of Prague Castle
- Catholic accounts credited divine intervention for their survival
- A rival story says they landed on a dung heap

@ Mysteries & Legends | Nine Hikers Who Fled Their Tent | Mysteries & Legends, Disasters
Nine Soviet hikers died in the Ural Mountains in 1959 after cutting their way out of their tent at night and fleeing into deep cold without proper clothing or boots.
- The group was led by Igor Dyatlov, and the pass now bears his name
- Six died of hypothermia; three had severe injuries
- The 1959 investigation blamed a "compelling natural force"
- Russia reopened the case and closed it again in 2020
- Officials concluded they fled an avalanche threat and got lost

@ Disasters | The Explosion That Left No Crater | Disasters, Space & Astronomy
In 1908 something from space exploded over Siberia with the force of a large nuclear weapon, flattening about 2,000 square kilometres of forest without leaving any crater.
- It happened near the Podkamennaya Tunguska River on 30 June 1908
- It blew apart 5 to 10 kilometres up, never reaching the ground
- It was probably a comet or asteroid
- Over 100 square kilometres of pine forest was charred
- The region was so remote that no expedition arrived for years

@ Food & Drink | Rome Ran on Rotten Fish Sauce | Food & Drink, Ancient History
The Romans' favourite condiment, garum, was made by letting fish decompose in salt for months, and a workshop making it was buried intact at Pompeii.
- Garum was used across the empire like we use ketchup or soy sauce
- Fish and salt were layered in vats and left in the sun
- Vesuvius buried the Pompeii workshop in 79 CE
- Researchers found vessels still holding fish bones and sauce residue
- Scholars have tried to recreate the recipe in modern labs

@ Inventions | The Ancient Greek Computer | Inventions, Space & Astronomy, Ancient History
A corroded lump of bronze gears recovered from a Greek shipwreck turned out to be a 2,000-year-old mechanical calculator that predicted eclipses and tracked the planets.
- It's called the Antikythera mechanism, after the island near the wreck
- It survives as 82 fragments containing 30 gears
- It dates to around the second century BCE
- It also kept a calendar and timed the Panhellenic games
- Nothing of comparable complexity is known for another thousand years
