@ Physics | The Experiment That Drips Once a Decade | Physics, Records, Eccentrics
A lump of pitch has been slowly dripping through a funnel at an Australian university since 1927, producing only nine drops in nearly a century, and it holds the record for the longest-running lab experiment.
- Pitch is a tar derivative that feels solid and shatters if struck
- It is about 100 billion times more viscous than water
- A physics professor set it up to show that it is really a fluid
- Nobody has ever actually witnessed a drop detach
- The ninth drop arrived in 2014

@ Economics & Trade | The Island That Mined Itself Away | Economics & Trade, Geography & Travel, Earth Science
Nauru, a Pacific island smaller than many airports, became one of the richest countries per head in the 1970s by digging up its own surface, which was made of ancient bird droppings.
- Seabird guano had built up into thick phosphate rock, a valuable fertiliser
- The money paid for a welfare state and royalties to residents
- Most of the island's interior was strip-mined into bare pinnacles
- Deposits were nearly exhausted by the early 2000s
- Mismanaged funds left the country indebted and aid-dependent
S PMC (Journal of Industrial Ecology) | 2019 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6411803/ | absurd-a66.html | phosphates made Nauru one of the world’s richest countries per capita during the 1970s

@ Medicine | The Women Who Painted With Radium | Medicine, Nuclear, Modern History
Factory workers in the 1920s painted glowing radium onto watch dials and were told to shape their brushes with their lips; many died, and five sued their employer for 250,000 dollars.
- Radium glows in the dark and was then thought harmless
- The women were nicknamed the "radium girls"
- Their jaws disintegrated from radiation absorbed into bone
- They settled for 10,000 dollars each plus annual payments
- None of the five survived more than two years after settling

@ Medicine | The Doctor Who Said Wash Your Hands | Medicine, Modern History
Ignaz Semmelweis showed in 1847 that disinfecting doctors' hands sharply cut deaths among new mothers, was rejected by his profession, and died in an asylum in 1865 after a beating.
- Childbed fever killed a large share of women giving birth in hospital
- Doctors were carrying contamination from autopsies to the maternity ward
- He had them wash in chlorinated lime, and deaths plummeted
- Germ theory did not yet exist to explain why it worked
- His wife and colleagues had him committed in 1865
