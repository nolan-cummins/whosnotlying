@ 2398 | Cooking Oil's Hidden Extinctions | Food & Drink, Plants | ETH Zurich, Switzerland
Growing crops for cooking oil has already put about 1.5% of the world's plant and land vertebrate species on a path to long-term loss, with rich countries' demand pushing most of that damage into the tropics.
- Oil crops include palm, soy, rapeseed and sunflower
- The analysis traced supply chains from 1995 to 2020
- Tropical oil crops replace the most species-rich habitats
- Consumers are often continents away from the loss
# oil crops, biodiversity loss, palm oil, supply chains, tropics, trade

@ 2400 | Rainforests Bounce Back in 30 Years | Plants, Animals | Technische Universität Darmstadt, Germany & Universidad de Las Américas, Ecuador
Tracking 16 groups of organisms across three kingdoms in recovering tropical forest showed abundance and diversity regained over 90% of old-growth levels within 30 years, though the exact mix of species took longer.
- About 60% of tropical forest has been lost or degraded
- Most recovery studies look only at trees
- Species composition reached about 75% similarity in 30 years
- Full recovery still takes several decades
# forest restoration, tropical rainforest, biodiversity recovery, Ecuador, ecosystem restoration

@ 2401 | Fewer People Didn't Save Japan's Wildlife | Animals, Geography & Travel | University of Sheffield, UK & Tokyo City University, Japan
Japan's shrinking population has not reversed biodiversity loss: most species studied kept declining whether local human numbers rose or fell, because farmland was either intensified or abandoned.
- Japan is among the first countries to depopulate substantially
- Global studies link species loss to population and economic growth
- Abandoned farmland loses the open habitats some species need
- Intensified farmland loses others
# depopulation, biodiversity, Japan, farmland abandonment, land use

@ 2406 | Borderlands Are Conservation Gaps | Animals, Law & Government | Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences
Mapping 19,039 land vertebrates showed three-quarters of the species ranges that fall along international borders lie outside any protected area.
- Border regions are often remote and lightly developed
- They are also politically sensitive and hard to manage jointly
- Protected areas rarely span two countries
- Many species' ranges straddle borders
# borderlands, protected areas, biodiversity, transboundary conservation, vertebrates

@ 2412 | Outsourced Parks Cut Deforestation | Plants, Law & Government | INRAE & CNRS, France
African protected areas run jointly by governments and conservation nonprofits lost far less forest since 2000 than comparable parks run by the state alone, especially the most threatened ones.
- Such arrangements are called collaborative management partnerships
- They bring outside funding, staff and training
- They have spread across sub-Saharan Africa since the 2000s
- Effects were measured by matching similar parks statistically
# protected areas, deforestation, Africa, conservation partnerships, park management

@ 2416 | The Black Death Grew Ancient Oaks | Plants, Medieval History | Università della Tuscia & University of Salento, Italy
Radiocarbon dating of Italy's oldest surviving oaks found many took root together from the early 1400s, as the Black Death emptied the countryside and released forests from grazing and cutting.
- The plague reached Europe in 1347 and recurred for centuries
- Depopulation reduced pressure on woodland
- Oaks established in that window are now the oldest standing
- Sites ranged from an island to high mountain slopes
# Black Death, oaks, rewilding, Mediterranean forests, radiocarbon dating, Italy

@ 2417 | Introduced Grazers Do More Harm Than Good | Animals, Plants | University of Fribourg, Switzerland & Université de Bordeaux, France
Assessing 2,021 recorded effects of introduced large plant-eating mammals worldwide found harms to native wildlife outnumbered and outweighed benefits, which were usually mild.
- Introduced herbivores include feral horses, camels and deer
- Some argue they replace extinct native grazers
- Harms often reduced whole populations, not just individuals
- Impacts were scored with standard IUCN frameworks
# introduced species, large herbivores, rewilding, biodiversity, invasive species
