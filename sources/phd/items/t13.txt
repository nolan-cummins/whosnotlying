@ 1180 | 700,000-Year-Old Squirrel Droppings | Ancient History, Genetics | Hakai Institute & McMaster University, Canada
Frozen ground squirrel droppings in Yukon permafrost, some 700,000 years old, preserved DNA from plants, insects, microbes and ice-age giants including mammoth, horse and steppe bison.
- Coprolites are fossilized or frozen droppings
- Permafrost keeps DNA from breaking down for very long periods
- The team assembled over 18 complete mitochondrial genomes
- One squirrel lineage was older than all living Arctic ground squirrel branches
- The droppings came from ancient burrows preserved in frozen ground

- Water scarcity means demand exceeding what's available
- The study worked month by month and sub-basin by sub-basin
- Peak-month demand is where unsustainable use concentrates

@ 1190 | Crops Soak Up China's Mercury | Agriculture, Chemistry | Huazhong Agricultural University, China
Wheat, rice and maize across China absorb about 25.6 tonnes of toxic mercury a year, roughly half from the air and half from soil, and 43% of it moves on into food and animal feed.
- Mercury is a toxic metal released by coal burning and mining
- Plant leaves absorb mercury vapor directly from the air
- Different mercury isotopes reveal whether it came from air or soil
- The survey covered 301 field sites
- Crop residues return 36% of the mercury to the soil
