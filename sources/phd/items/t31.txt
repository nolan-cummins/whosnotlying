@ 2385 | Solar Panels Over Fish Ponds | Energy, Food & Drink | Ministry of Natural Resources, China
Covering the world's freshwater fish farms partly with solar panels could generate large amounts of power while offsetting the greenhouse gases those farms emit, a satellite-based global assessment found.
- Inland fish farming has expanded rapidly worldwide
- Fish ponds release methane and nitrous oxide
- Panels over water shade it and stay cooler themselves
- The combination is called aquavoltaics
# aquavoltaics, aquaculture, solar power, greenhouse gases, food-energy nexus

@ 2389 | People Fish the Oddest-Shaped Fish | Marine Life, Food & Drink | CNRS & INP Toulouse, France
Humans use about a quarter of the world's freshwater fish species, and those species cover almost half of all fish body shapes, meaning unusual forms and the ecological roles they fill are hit hardest.
- Body shape reflects what a fish eats and where it lives
- Different uses target different shapes: food, aquarium, sport
- Losing unique forms removes functions nothing else performs
- 26% of species are used; they span 45% of shape diversity
# freshwater fish, morphological diversity, fishing, functional erosion, biodiversity

@ 2387 | Britain Exports Its Own Nutrition | Food & Drink, Economics & Trade | University of Aberdeen, UK
Britain exports so much oily fish that it sends abroad omega-3 fats equal to 73% of its own recommended intake, while failing to meet national fish-eating guidelines at home.
- Oily fish like salmon and mackerel carry omega-3, vitamin B12 and vitamin D
- The UK exports much of its catch and farmed salmon
- It imports different species, often white fish and shrimp
- Exports also carry away 46% of recommended B12 intake
# seafood, exports, omega-3, nutrition, United Kingdom, food systems
